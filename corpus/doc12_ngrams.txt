Character n-grams break a word into short overlapping sequences. They help a retrieval system compare similar spellings and generate candidates for query correction. A bigram representation of a word includes pairs of neighbouring letters, often with boundary markers at the beginning and end.

For example, two similar spellings share many of their bigrams even when one letter is missing or substituted. The system can compare the n-gram sets using Jaccard similarity: the number of shared n-grams divided by the number of n-grams in either set. A higher score suggests that two strings are structurally similar.

N-grams are also useful for wildcard retrieval and approximate matching. A search engine may use them to limit a huge vocabulary to a smaller set of likely candidates before calculating edit distance. In this project the n-gram function is used as a transparent secondary comparison for spelling suggestions, following the more direct Levenshtein-distance ordering.
